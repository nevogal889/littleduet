## Core loop

Watch a short instructor video segment, then play that segment on a connected keyboard against scrolling notation with live green/red note feedback and a score. Pass the section, and the next one unlocks. Bootcamp first, then songs and Courses.

## Teaching & video mechanism

### 1. Lesson format: what a lesson is made of

- **Video segment followed by an interactive exercise, repeated.** Lessons are "divided between videos to watch, and playing exercises to follow" (source 4). "You essentially get taught what to do by an instructor in a video," then practice what you learned against the software (source 5). The launch design paired "video demonstrations" with "interactive sheet music" (source 2).
- **Video length:** 5–10 minutes, or 5–15 in the same article's FAQ (source 6). Sections take "anywhere from 3 to 30 minutes" to complete (source 4).
- **Instructors:** a small cast of real musicians, mainly David Sides and Phil Anderson (source 5), with Harry Connick Jr. also cited (source 12). Quincy Jones co-created the curriculum (sources 1, 2).
- **The play screen** is a split view: sheet music, an on-screen keyboard highlighting the notes to play, and the instructor (source 6). Overlays include finger numbers and note labels (sources 3, 4).
- **Song lessons are chunked by song structure:** intro, verse, pre-chorus, chorus. Within each chunk the right hand comes first, then the left, then both together (sources 3, 4, 6). "The lessons are taught within a song" (source 12).
- **Three performance contexts:** Practice mode (solo with real-time visual feedback), band play (with arranged accompaniment), and Performance Mode (play, record, share) (source 8).
- **Unit structure (Bootcamp):** Rookie 93 sections, Intermediate 63, Advanced 25 (sources 4, 5). Intermediate "starts with the basics of piano chords before ending with chord progressions"; Advanced covers voicings and arpeggios (source 5). Courses (30-day challenge, octave scales, theory, improvisation) are supplementary and "unlock when you hit certain performance goals" (sources 3, 5).

### 2. Input and feedback

- **MIDI first, microphone second.** USB/MIDI cable gives "the highest accuracy". The device microphone is an opt-in **beta** "cable-free" option (source 1, current listing). "Microphone recognition is secondary to MIDI" (10). Older reviews said a MIDI keyboard was required and acoustic pianos were unsupported (3, 9).
- **Live note colouring:** correct notes turn green, wrong notes turn red (3, 4, 5, 6, 12). A right note in the wrong rhythm shows **pink** (7).
- **Wait behaviour:** "The notes you get incorrect turn red and prevent you from playing on" (5). *Inference (medium-low): some mode halts on a wrong note, wait-mode style.* No source names a "Wait Mode", so it is **unverified as a named feature**. The feedback colouring can be toggled off (5).
- **Count-in:** a voice and metronome count you in when you press Play (4). One user asked that "the count in voice should be at the same tempo as the music" (reviews.csv).
- **Scoring:** after each attempt, the app shows a percentage or accuracy rating plus points (3, 4). It also tracks improvement against your last attempt and flags problem areas (2, launch design).
- **Pass gate:** "you only progress once you get all the notes right in this section" (6). A user reports it "essentially demands 100% note accuracy and 90% timing or you fail" (Trustpilot, reviews.csv). The exact thresholds are **unverified first-party**.
- **Practice tools:** tempo adjust, loop a selected passage, hands separate, metronome, backing track/mixer, record yourself, on-screen keyboard (3, 5, 9, 12). Loop markers are hard to place precisely (Trustpilot 2026).
- **Latency:** a 2026 reviewer says a rewrite to Electron removed low-latency driver selection (reviews.csv).

### 3. Curriculum and pacing

- **Method: play-first through popular songs.** Notation and theory are woven in, but playing comes before reading. "If you're hoping to learn to read music before you get playing, this isn't the site for you" (3). The 5-finger position comes in the second lesson (3). Bootcamp covers "chord progressions, notation and key signatures" (2).
- **No ear-first, solfège or rhythm-syllable pedagogy** appears in any source. Rhythm is taught through the metronome, count-in and timing grading.
- **What comes next:** a linear Bootcamp sequence, gated by passing each section (6). Courses unlock on performance goals (3, 5). Placement and skipping are weak: "I wish I could fast forward to a find my level" (Trustpilot). "Some of the boot camp lessons are painfully slow" (Trustpilot). "Dont know where to start if youre a beginner" (Trustpilot).
- **Song arrangements in three difficulty levels** (Rookie, Intermediate, Advanced) for the 2,000+ song library (1, 4). Experienced players can jump straight to "Full Performance" (4).
- **Review / spaced practice:** no evidence of scheduled review. Repetition is learner-driven: "you can repeat these exercises as many times as necessary until you get them right" (4).

### 4. Guidance

- **The instructor demonstrates on video, then plays along with the student** (8). Hands are taught separately before they are combined (3).
- **Onboarding is a weak spot:** "The program has no instructions how to use it." "I did not know I was to notify the program that I had connected it by usb." "I received my order but didn't receive information on how to access lessons" (Trustpilot, 4 of 40 sampled). Users also asked for a visible map of the whole journey: "I WANT A MANUAL that has a MAP that SHOWS THE ENTIRE JOURNEY FROM ROOKIE TO ADVANCE" (Trustpilot).
- **No parent-coaching layer found.** The Educational App Store reviewer notes instant feedback "helps parents with limited piano knowledge" (9). That is the only parent framing.

### 5. Kids and parent angle

- **Built for teens and adults.** "Playground Sessions is designed for adults" (3). Geared "toward older children and teens" (11). The Educational App Store lists 11–18 (9). The company reportedly says "a child over 7 can use the platform", and the reviewer thinks about 10+ (5). PianoDreamers says 7+ (4). The App Store age rating of 4+ is a content rating, not a skill target.
- **Families:** family lifetime plans for two or more people (5), a "family plan" (7), and "love that my whole family can learn together" (Trustpilot). One parent bought it for her kids, who "would have preferred a more game-like approach" (Trustpilot).
- **Homeschool:** listed among the target audiences in the App Store description (1). No dedicated homeschool product was found.

### 6. Motivation

- **Points → levels:** points from each graded attempt accumulate toward the next level. The dashboard shows points needed (3, 4).
- **Stars, badges, achievements:** badges for accuracy, rhythm and other skills (5, 8). The App Store lists "Points, rewards, levels, and achievements" (1). Under the older model, 15 stars earned free song credits (3).
- **Progress dashboard ("My Playground"):** total points, time played, overall accuracy, completed and attempted classes, progress charts over time (3, 4, 6, 8).
- **Social:** at one time students could network with others to practise, chat or challenge each other (8, undated). Current status is unverified.

### 7. Monetization

- **Subscription plus lifetime;** the free tier is a trial and some free songs. Prices vary by source and year:
  - 2025: $24.99/month, $12.49/month billed annually, $349.99 lifetime (4).
  - 2025: $19.99/month, $119.99/year, $289.99 lifetime (7).
  - 2022: $17.99/month, $119.88/year, $289.99 lifetime (3).
  - Launch (2013): $9.99/month (2).
- **Song access:** this used to work as credits (5 free songs a month, then $1.99 each) (3, 5, 12). Newer sources describe unlimited access to 2,000+ songs (4, 7). Lifetime's only extra perk is "2 years of unlimited access" to songs (4).
- **Guarantees and bundles:** a 30-day money-back guarantee (4, 6) and keyboard bundles (7; Trustpilot "$240, a fair deal for a beginner piano and lessons").
- The official pricing page could not be read, so **current prices are unverified first-party.**

## Flows

```
F01 Learner completes a Bootcamp section
    S01 -> S02 video -> S03 play (count-in, play, colour feedback) -> S04 result -> pass ? next section : retry S03
    happy path clicks: ~4 per section
    edge: no MIDI connected; mic beta inaccurate; must hit all notes to pass; loop markers imprecise
F02 Learn a song
    S05 pick song + level -> S02/S03 per chunk (RH, LH, both; intro/verse/chorus) -> Full Performance
```

## Lessons for Little Duet

Ideas only. Do not copy their songs, arrangements, videos, instructor personas or wording.

Worth learning from:
1. **Interleave short video with an immediate "your turn".** Their segment → play → result rhythm is the core mechanism. For ages 2–6, make each turn 10–30 seconds (*inference*).
2. **Chunk songs by phrase and by hand, then combine.** Their structure-chunk plus right-hand / left-hand / both ladder maps directly onto parent part + child part, then together.
3. **Same song, multiple arrangements.** The duet version of this: the child plays a 1–3 key part while the parent plays the full accompaniment. The parent becomes the backing band.
4. **A three-state colour language** (right, wrong, right note but off the beat) is a clear model. For toddlers, consider positive-only cues: glow on correct, gentle nudge in place of red (*inference*).
5. **Wait-on-wrong-note behaviour** suits beginners. The song pauses until the right key is pressed. That is far kinder than a fail grade for a 3-year-old.
6. **Show the whole journey map.** Users explicitly asked for one.

Avoid:
1. **Hard pass gates** (all notes / 90% timing). For ages 2–6, never block progress on accuracy. Let the parent advance.
2. **Hardware friction as a precondition.** MIDI cables, "notify the program" setup and acoustic-piano exclusion. If Little Duet listens, the mic must be the primary path, with a no-detection "parent says done" fallback.
3. **Adult framing.** Their reviewers and age guidance put the floor at 7–10+. Nothing in their UX coaches a parent. This is Little Duet's open space.
4. **Slow, unskippable videos and weak placement.** Make every video skippable and offer a "we already know this one" path.
5. **Small notation.** Pre-readers need big keys and colours, not staff notation.
6. **Billing opacity:** codes not honoured, hard-to-buy lifetime plans. Keep plans simple and the cancel path easy. One positive review praised exactly that.
