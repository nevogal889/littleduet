# Little Duet — Compliance rules for features A–E

Research compiled 8 November 2026. **This is research, not legal advice.** Every claim links to its source. Where the rule text is decisive it is quoted. "Needs a lawyer" marks points where the sources do not settle the question.

**Baseline being assessed:** iOS Kids Category (age band "5 and under") plus Google Play Families. Mic listening happens on the device only and audio is never recorded or sent. The child's nickname and birthday stay on the device. A parent account (email/Apple/Google) is optional and syncs progress through Supabase. RevenueCat runs with an anonymous app user id (per `NOTES-app.md`). There are no ads and no analytics SDKs. A parental gate guards purchases, links, the account and the Grown-ups area. A streak exists, with "no streak notifications."

**New features:** A adaptive AI teacher · B weekly parent report · C singing/clapping/movement games (mic, maybe camera) · D family/social (grandparents, recorded duet, family concert) · E first-party analytics.

## 1. US — COPPA Rule (16 CFR Part 312), as amended in 2025

### 1.1 Status as of November 2026
- The final rule was published 22 April 2025 (90 FR 16918). From the DATES section: "The amended Rule is effective June 23, 2025" and "regulated entities have until April 22, 2026 to comply." https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-04-22/html/2025-05904.htm · https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/22/2025-05904/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule
- **The rule is now fully in force.** The obligations that took effect 22 April 2026 are: a written information-security program; vendor due diligence with written assurances; expanded direct and online notices that include a written data retention policy; **separate consent for third-party disclosure**; deletion once data is no longer needed; and determining whether users of mixed-audience services are children. https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/ftc-prioritizes-coppa-enforcement-new-compliance-obligations-take-effect
- **2026 update, 25 February 2026:** the FTC issued an age-verification policy statement. It promises non-enforcement for data collected *solely* for age verification. It **does not cover services primarily directed to children**, so it does not help Little Duet. The FTC also says a further COPPA amendment on age verification is planned, with no timeline. https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/ftc-prioritizes-coppa-enforcement-new-compliance-obligations-take-effect · https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-03-02-ftc-issues-coppa-enforcement-discretion-policy-to-incentivize-use-of-age-verification-technologies · https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule-coppa
- FTC officials have called children's privacy an enforcement priority and say more cases are coming "in the coming months and years." https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/ftc-prioritizes-coppa-enforcement-new-compliance-obligations-take-effect
- On AI chatbots, the FTC issued 6(b) orders on 11 September 2025 to seven chatbot companies, asking about effects on children and COPPA compliance. https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/ai-law-and-regulation-tracker/ftc-investigates-chatbots-impact-on-children

### 1.2 What counts as personal information (§312.2, amended text)
Quoted from the codified amendments (https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-04-22/html/2025-05904.htm):
- (7) "A persistent identifier that can be used to recognize a user over time and across different websites or online services." The examples given are a customer number held in a cookie, **an IP address**, a device serial number and a unique device identifier.
- (8) "A photograph, video, or audio file where such file contains a child's image or voice;"
- (10) "A biometric identifier that can be used for the automated or semi-automated recognition of an individual". The listed examples include **voiceprints, facial templates/faceprints and gait patterns**. The final rule dropped the proposed "data derived from voice data, gait data, or facial data." https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/ftc-publishes-final-coppa-rule-amendments
- (11) "Information concerning the child or the parents of that child that the operator collects online from the child" and combines with an identifier.
- The FTC declined to treat avatars generated from a child's image as personal information. https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/coppas-coming-of-age-key-compliance-changes-in-ftcs-final-rule

### 1.3 The voice-recording exception (§312.5(c)(9))
- Rule text: "Where an operator collects an audio file containing a child's voice, and no other personal information," the operator may use it to respond to the child's specific request. It must not use or disclose the file for anything else, and it "deletes it immediately after responding to the child's request". The online notice must describe the practice (§312.4(d)(4)). https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-04-22/html/2025-05904.htm
- This codifies the 2017 policy, which applies only where audio is used "solely as a replacement for written words, such as to perform a search or fulfill a verbal instruction or request." It also says "the operator may not make any other use of the audio file," including "behavioral targeting or profiling purposes." It does not apply where the operator asks for personal information by voice, such as a name. https://ftc.gov/system/files/documents/public_statements/1266473/coppa_policy_statement_audiorecordings.pdf
- **Implication:** cloud analysis of a child's playing or singing in order to assess and adapt a lesson is *not* a "replacement for written words." Do not rely on this exception for a cloud AI teacher. **(Needs a lawyer if you plan to rely on it at all.)**

### 1.4 Separate consent for disclosure to third parties (§312.5(a)(2))
- The parent must be able to consent to collection and use without consenting to third-party disclosure, unless the disclosure is integral to the service. "An operator required to give the parent this option must obtain separate verifiable parental consent to such disclosure." https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-04-22/html/2025-05904.htm
- The FTC states that disclosures "to train or otherwise develop artificial intelligence technologies, are not integral to the website or online service" and "would require consent." https://www.dataprotectionreport.com/2025/06/ftcs-coppa-rule-changes-include-ai-training-consent-requirement/
- The FTC has not defined "integral." https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/ftc-prioritizes-coppa-enforcement-new-compliance-obligations-take-effect
- A service provider that only supports internal operations and "does not use or disclose information protected under this part for any other purpose" is not a "third party" (§312.2). https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-04-22/html/2025-05904.htm

### 1.5 Support for internal operations (the analytics exception)
- §312.2 lists (i) "maintaining or analyzing the functioning" of the service, (iii) "authenticating users or personalizing content", (v) security and integrity, and (vi) legal compliance, among others. Such data "cannot be used or disclosed to contact a specific individual, including through behavioral advertising," or to build a profile. https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-04-22/html/2025-05904.htm
- §312.5(c)(7) allows a persistent identifier with no other personal information, solely for internal operations, **without parental consent**. The notice must list "the specific internal operations for which the operator has collected a persistent identifier" (§312.4(d)(3)). The same source covers both points.
- The FTC **dropped** its proposed ban on using persistent identifiers to prompt engagement through push notifications or in-app nudges: the final rule "reversed course and rejected that addition." https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/coppas-coming-of-age-key-compliance-changes-in-ftcs-final-rule

### 1.6 Retention, security and the parent contact exception
- §312.10: data may be kept "for only as long as is reasonably necessary". "Personal information collected online from a child may not be retained indefinitely." The operator must keep "a written data retention policy" that sets out "the business need for retaining such information, and a timeframe for deletion of such information", and publish it in the online notice. https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-04-22/html/2025-05904.htm
- §312.8 requires "a written information security program" with a designated coordinator, at least annual risk assessments, and testing. https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-04-22/html/2025-05904.htm · https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/ftc-publishes-final-coppa-rule-amendments
- §312.5(c)(2) lets an operator collect a parent's contact information to give voluntary notice and later updates about the child's participation, but **only where the service otherwise collects no personal information from the child**. https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-04-22/html/2025-05904.htm

### 1.7 What COPPA means for Little Duet
- **Existing gap to check now (needs a lawyer):** Supabase sync links the child's progress (information "concerning the child") to an account id and a parent email. That is likely personal information collected from a child. If so, sync needs verifiable parental consent (VPC) through a recognised method, not just a parental gate. Apple says so directly: "the parental gate requirement … is generally not the same as securing parental consent" (5.1.4). https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/ The RevenueCat anonymous app user id is a persistent identifier, so it should be documented as internal operations (purchase fulfilment), with written assurances from RevenueCat (§312.8).
- **On-device-only processing:** the 2013 rule says "collects" covers passive tracking "irrespective of the technology used." https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/client-advisories/ftc-issues-final-amendments-to-the-childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule-coppa-a-detailed-look-at-whats-changed.md I found **no FTC statement** that processing on the device and never transmitted is not "collection." This is the main design bet, so get written legal sign-off.

## 2. App store rules

### 2.1 Apple App Store (guidelines last updated 16 Oct 2026; https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **1.3 Kids Category:** "These apps must not include links out of the app, purchasing opportunities, or other distractions to kids unless reserved for a designated area behind a parental gate." "Kids Category apps may not send personally identifiable information or device information to third parties. Apps in the Kids Category should not include third-party analytics or third-party advertising." There is a narrow exception: "third-party analytics may be permitted provided that the services do not collect or transmit the IDFA or any identifiable information about children … their location, or their devices. This includes any device, network, or other information that could be used directly or combined with other information to identify users and their devices." Also: "once customers expect your app to follow the Kids Category requirements, it will need to continue to meet these guidelines in subsequent updates, even if you decide to deselect the category."
- **5.1.4(a):** "Apps intended primarily for kids should not include third-party analytics or third-party advertising." **5.1.4(b):** apps that "collect, transmit, or have the capability to share personal information (such as name, address, email, location, photos, videos, drawings, the ability to chat …) from a minor must include a privacy policy and must comply with all applicable children's privacy statutes."
- **5.1.2(i), third-party AI:** "You must clearly disclose where personal data will be shared with third parties, including with third-party AI, and obtain explicit permission before doing so."
- **2.5.14, recording:** "Apps must request explicit user consent and provide a clear visual and/or audible indication when recording, logging, or otherwise making a record of user activity. This includes any use of the device camera, microphone".
- **1.2, user-generated content** (this "includes … AI content creation apps"). Such apps must have filtering, reporting with "timely responses," blocking, and published contact information.
- **5.1.1(v):** "If your app supports account creation, you must also offer account deletion within the app."
- **Kids Category developer page:** parental gates apply to purchases and link-outs, and the page recommends "a voiceover prompt" for pre-literate children. "Kids apps should not transmit personally identifiable information or device information to third parties — even in sections intended for adults — unless the parent explicitly consents." The page also describes PermissionKit, which lets a child ask a parent before communicating with others, and the Declared Age Range API. https://developer.apple.com/app-store/kids-apps/?utm_id=DP
- **AI:** Apple has no rule specific to AI in kids apps beyond 5.1.2(i) and 1.2. I found none on the Kids page (same URL).

### 2.2 Google Play Families (https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335)
- Apps must disclose collection of children's personal and sensitive information, which explicitly includes "microphone and camera sensor data."
- Apps that solely target children "must not transmit Android advertising identifier (AAID), SIM Serial, Build Serial, BSSID, MAC, SSID, IMEI, and/or IMSI." They "may not request location permission, or collect, use, and transmit precise location." They "should not request AD_ID permission".
- "Apps that solely target children must not contain any APIs or SDKs that are not approved for use" in child-directed services. Ads may come only from Families self-certified ads SDKs.
- **Social features:** apps must show "an in-app reminder to be safe online" before children exchange freeform media, and "require adult action" (a PIN, password or birthdate) before children exchange personal information. Adults must be able to manage the features.
- **Compliance:** apps "including any APIs or SDKs" must comply with COPPA and the GDPR.
- **AI-Generated Content policy:** "Apps that generate content using AI must contain in-app user reporting or flagging features". Restricted content, including child-endangerment content, is prohibited. https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13985936
- **Teacher Approved:** reviewers rate design, appeal, "enrichment," age-appropriateness, and how "ads, in-app purchases, and cross-promotion affect the overall quality." https://play.google.com/console/about/programs/teacherapproved
- **Engineering note:** per `NOTES-app.md`, the Android build currently blocks the mic and camera permissions. Features A and C need the manifest and the Data safety form updated.

## 3. UK Age Appropriate Design Code and EU GDPR/DSA

### 3.1 UK Children's Code (ICO; https://ico.org.uk/media/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services-2-1.pdf)
- **Std 7, defaults:** "Settings must be 'high privacy' by default".
- **Std 8, minimisation:** collect only what is needed for elements "in which a child is actively and knowingly engaged". "Give children separate choices over which elements they wish to activate."
- **Std 9, sharing:** "Do not disclose children's data unless you can demonstrate a compelling reason to do so".
- **Std 10, geolocation:** "Switch geolocation options off by default". "Options which make a child's location visible to others must default back to 'off' at the end of each session."
- **Std 11, parental controls:** "provide an obvious sign to the child when they are being monitored."
- **Std 12, profiling:** "Switch options which use profiling 'off' by default". Profiling that is essential to the core service is read narrowly. Light "personalisation of a 'walled garden'" is relatively benign. Personalised extras that are not core get their own setting, off by default.
- **Std 13, nudges:** "Do not use nudge techniques to lead or encourage children to provide unnecessary personal data". The code encourages nudges towards privacy and wellbeing and "pause and save buttons". On detrimental use: "avoid features which use personal data to automatically extend use".
- **Ages 0–5:** "pre-literate and early literacy". The code suggests audio or video prompts telling children to get a parent if they try to change privacy settings.
- **2025–26:** the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 adds UK GDPR Art 25(1A)–(1B), so information society services must take into account "the children's higher protection matters." https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/18/section/81 The ICO is monitoring mobile games (from December 2025) on default privacy, geolocation and ad profiling, and is reviewing DPIAs. https://www.mondaq.com/uk/privacy-protection/1768616/the-ico-rates-the-top-ten-most-popular-mobile-games-on-the-protection-of-childrens-privacy
- **Streaks and notifications:** the code governs how the service treats the *child*. Notifications sent only to a parent's device are outside the child-nudge concern, but the streak shown to the child must not "automatically extend use." The current "never says lost / no streak notifications" design fits that.

### 3.2 EU GDPR
- **Art 8(1):** for information society services offered directly to a child, consent-based processing is lawful only from age 16 (Member States may set 13–16). Below that age, only "if and to the extent that consent is given or authorised by the holder of parental responsibility." **Art 8(2):** "The controller shall make reasonable efforts to verify … taking into consideration available technology." https://gdpr-text.com/en/read/article-8/
- **Voice:** "voice data is inherently biometric personal data". Using it for identification is Art 9 special-category data and needs explicit consent. "Voice models should be generated, stored and matched exclusively on the local device." (EDPB Guidelines 02/2021, paras 31, 128, 133.) https://www.edpb.europa.eu:443/system/files/2021-07/edpb_guidelines_202102_on_vva_v2.0_adopted_en.pdf
- **SDK and telemetry storage/access on the device:** ePrivacy Art 5(3) reaches beyond cookies to "unique identifiers," "IP-only tracking" and "local processing" use cases (EDPB Guidelines 2/2023, adopted 16 Oct 2024). https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/edpb-adopts-guidelines-on-scope-of-eprivacy-directive **Needs a lawyer:** whether privacy-preserving first-party analytics needs ePrivacy consent in each target Member State. The UK DUAA relaxes this for "certain, low-risk situations" (https://www.gov.uk/guidance/data-use-and-access-act-2025-data-protection-and-privacy-changes), but I did not verify the exact scope.

### 3.3 EU DSA
- The Art 28 guidelines (July 2025) call for private-by-default accounts, and say engagement features ("communication 'streaks'", push notifications, autoplay) should be off by default for minors. They also call for safeguards on integrated AI chatbots, and say "Self-declaration does not constitute appropriate age assurance." https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/insights-and-events/insights/2025/07/rd-european-commission-guidelines-on-protection-of-minors-under-the-digital-services-act · https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/commission-publishes-guidelines-protection-minors
- **Scope:** these duties apply to *online platforms*, meaning services that disseminate user content to the public. Art 19(1) exempts "providers of online platforms that qualify as micro or small enterprises". https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/dsa/dsa-regulation/article-19/ A closed family circle is probably not "dissemination to the public," and Little Duet is small. **Needs a lawyer** before any public "concert." Even so, the guidelines are the best statement of regulator expectations.

## 4. EU AI Act (Reg. 2024/1689, as amended by the Digital Omnibus on AI)
- **Status:** the Omnibus (Reg. 2026/1744) entered into force 27 July 2026. Annex III high-risk obligations move to **2 December 2027**. Art 50 transparency applies from **2 August 2026**, with marking for systems already on the market by **2 December 2026**. A new prohibition covers AI that generates non-consensual intimate imagery and CSAM. https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/eu-digital-omnibus-on-ai-enters-into-force · https://www.joneswalker.com/en/insights/blogs/ai-law-blog/yes-august-2-still-matters-the-eu-approved-a-high-risk-ai-delay-but-most-trans.html
- **Art 5(1)(b), in force since Feb 2025:** prohibits AI that "exploits any of the vulnerabilities of a natural person or a specific group of persons due to their age" so as to materially distort behaviour, causing or likely to cause significant harm. **Art 5(1)(a)** prohibits manipulative or deceptive techniques on the same harm standard. https://www.artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/5/ An adaptive teacher tuned to maximise session time for 2–6-year-olds is the risky pattern here.
- **Art 5(1)(f):** prohibits the "use of AI systems to infer emotions of a natural person in the areas of workplace and education institutions" except for medical or safety reasons (same URL). A home app is not an "education institution," but selling into preschools or kindergartens would trigger this. Outside institutions, **Art 50(3)** still requires deployers of emotion recognition to inform those exposed.
- **Annex III point 3 (high-risk, education):** covers AI "to evaluate learning outcomes," including steering the learning process. https://www.artificialintelligenceact.eu/annex/3/ The official text frames it around educational and vocational training *institutions*. The fetched page did not let me confirm that wording verbatim, so **needs a lawyer** if Little Duet is ever sold B2B to schools. A consumer home app is likely outside, but this is not settled.
- **Art 50(1):** AI systems that "interact directly with natural persons" must be designed so the interaction is "obvious." **Art 50(2):** providers of generative systems must mark synthetic audio "in a machine-readable format." **Art 50(5):** disclose "at the latest at the time of the first interaction." https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-50 For a 2–6-year-old, "obvious" is hard to meet, so disclose to the parent and make the character plainly non-human. **Needs a lawyer** on how Art 50(1) applies to pre-literate users.

## 5. US state laws, 2025–2026
- **App Store Accountability Acts:** Texas SB 2420 is in effect after the Fifth Circuit stayed the injunction on 28 May 2026. The Supreme Court denied emergency applications on 6 July 2026 ("not a ruling on the merits"). Developers must ingest the store's age category and consent signal, use it only for permitted purposes, notify the store of "significant changes," and delete the data after use. Utah takes effect 6 May 2027, Louisiana 1 July 2027, Alabama 1 January 2027. California's Digital Age Assurance Act (OS-level signal) takes effect 1 January 2027. https://www.ashurstperkinscoie.com/en/insights/supreme-court-green-lights-the-texas-age-verification-law-for-app-stores/ · https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2026/06/childrens-online-privacy-2026-state-app-store-design-code-and-social-media-laws
  - **Apple:** use the Declared Age Range API, the PermissionKit Significant Change API, StoreKit `ageRatingCode` and server notifications. "It's the developer's responsibility to determine when there's a significant change". "a change in the age rating of an app" counts as one. https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=2ezb6jhj **Google:** Play Age Signals API; significant changes can be declared in Play Console. https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16569691
  - **Little Duet:** adding AI, camera or family sharing is very likely a "significant change," so expect Texas parents to be asked to re-consent. There is no size exemption for developers in these summaries.
- **California AADC (AB 2273):** on 12 March 2026 the Ninth Circuit narrowed the injunction. High-privacy defaults, child-appropriate disclosures, monitoring and geolocation signals, and rights tools are now enforceable. The DPIA, the data-use restrictions in §31(b)(1)–(4) and the dark-patterns ban remain enjoined. https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-03-30-netchoice-v-bonta-ninth-circuit-narrows-injunction-against-californias-ageappropriate-design-code-act Loeb describes the data-use status differently (same Loeb URL above), so verify. The CAADC uses the CCPA definitions in §1798.140 (https://california.public.law/codes/civil_code_section_1798.99.30). That means it reaches only CCPA "businesses," which have revenue or data-volume thresholds; **needs a lawyer** to confirm whether Little Duet qualifies today.
- **Other design codes and kids' privacy laws with no or low thresholds:** Connecticut SB 1295 (1 July 2026, "No minimum business threshold"), Vermont (1 January 2027, profiling ban and duty of care against "compulsive use"), Nebraska (amended 2026 to drop its revenue threshold), South Carolina (in effect, litigation pending), Maryland, Colorado and Montana. https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2026/06/childrens-online-privacy-2026-state-app-store-design-code-and-social-media-laws These generally require high-privacy defaults, no dark patterns, profiling limits, and in some cases DPIAs.
- **California SB 243 (companion chatbots, effective 1 January 2026):** when a user is known to be a minor, the operator must disclose AI, show break reminders "at least every three hours," and block sexual content. The law includes a private right of action. Game bots are excluded only if "their responses are limited to replies about the video game." https://perkinscoie.com/insights/update/california-companion-chatbot-law-now-effect A piano teacher with a scripted persona that does not build a relationship is likely outside, but **needs a lawyer** if it remembers the child or chats freely.

## 6. Practical patterns that keep the app compliant (sources above)
1. **On-device first.** Pitch, rhythm and pose inference run on the device. No raw audio, video or features are persisted or transmitted. Show a visible "listening" or "camera on" indicator (Apple 2.5.14; ICO Std 11).
2. **Parent-consented cloud tier.** Anything that leaves the device (sync, reports, family sharing) sits behind a parent account with VPC (COPPA §312.5) and GDPR Art 8 parental consent, with per-feature toggles (ICO Std 8). Each disclosure to a third party is a separate toggle (§312.5(a)(2)).
3. **No child persistent ids for analytics.** Aggregate counts on the device and send coarse buckets with no device id. Drop IPs at the edge. Use a minimum count per bucket. Use a persistent id only under the §312.5(c)(7) internal-operations exception, named in the notice (§312.4(d)(3)).
4. **Parent-facing outputs.** Reports, notifications and nudges go to the parent's channel only, and are opt-in and off by default (DSA guidelines; ICO Std 7).
5. **Closed family circle.** Members are invited and approved by the parent, with no discovery, no public sharing, and no child-initiated contact. Show Google's "be safe online" reminder and require adult action. Use PermissionKit on iOS.
6. **Written policies.** Retention schedule with deletion timeframes (§312.10), security program (§312.8), vendor assurances, a DPIA (ICO; AADC states).

## 8. Design rules for Little Duet

1. **Nothing the child produces leaves the device by default.** Mic and camera inference is on-device, frames and audio are discarded right after inference, and nothing is written to disk. Legal sign-off is needed that this is not COPPA "collection."
2. **One consent gate, then per-feature switches.** Cloud sync, email reports, family circle and cloud AI each need a parent account with a recognised COPPA VPC method (not the math gate) plus GDPR Art 8 consent. Each feature has its own toggle, all off by default.
3. **Third parties need their own consent.** Every disclosure that is not integral (any AI vendor that could train, any sharing outside the family) gets its own consent screen (§312.5(a)(2)) and Apple's "explicit permission" for third-party AI (5.1.2(i)). Vendor contracts state no training, no secondary use, and written security assurances.
4. **The AI teacher speaks from a vetted script.** Adaptation picks from pre-written lines rendered by TTS ahead of time. No free-form LLM output reaches the child. The parent sees "AI-adapted lesson / synthetic voice" in Grown-ups. A report-a-problem flag sits in Grown-ups to cover the Google AI-content policy.
5. **No identification, no emotion inference.** No voiceprints, faceprints or gait templates. No inferring mood or frustration. Tempo and difficulty adapt from accuracy only.
6. **Adaptation serves learning, not time on app.** Do not optimise for session length or return rate. Classes end at natural stopping points, with pause and save that keeps progress (ICO detrimental use; AI Act 5(1)(a)/(b)).
7. **Parent-only notifications.** Reports and reminders go only to the parent's email or device, opt-in. The child never gets a push, and streak copy never shows loss (keep the current rule).
8. **A closed family circle that the parent administers.** Invites are created behind the gate, adults are approved by the parent, and there is no discovery, public link, comments or child-initiated contact. Show the "be safe online" reminder, require adult action, and offer report and block (Google Families; Apple 1.2).
9. **The parent exports recordings.** Duet recordings stay on the device unless the parent exports them through the gated share sheet. Any cloud copy needs VPC, a deletion date (for example 30 days, set in the retention policy), and in-app delete.
10. **Analytics with no identity.** Daily on-device aggregates (classes started and finished, error buckets, crash signatures) are sent without device ids. IPs are stripped at the edge. A bucket is reported only if it reaches a minimum count. No analytics SDKs.
11. **Paperwork before launch.** Written retention policy with timeframes (§312.10), a written infosec program (§312.8), a DPIA covering every new feature (ICO; Connecticut, Vermont and Nebraska design codes), updated privacy policy notices including the §312.4(d)(3) internal-operations list, Apple privacy label, and Google Data safety form.
12. **Treat each new feature as a "significant change."** Integrate the Declared Age Range API, PermissionKit Significant Change and Play Age Signals. Re-run the age rating for each new feature. Keep the Kids-Category promise (Apple 1.3 says the requirements persist even if the category is deselected).

## 9. Open legal questions (for counsel)
1. Does processing on the device that is never transmitted or stored count as COPPA "collection"? Same question for GDPR processing and ePrivacy Art 5(3) "access."
2. Does today's Supabase progress sync (account id plus progress, no name) already require VPC? Does the RevenueCat anonymous id fit §312.5(c)(7)?
3. Which VPC method should be used (for example card plus notice, or the new "text plus" or face-match methods)? Does Apple or Google sign-in count?
4. What is "integral" for a cloud AI vendor bound by contract to no training? Is it a "third party" at all under §312.2?
5. How does AI Act Art 50(1) disclosure work for pre-literate users? Is Little Duet the "provider" under Art 50(2) if it uses a vendor's TTS?
6. Would a B2B or preschool channel bring in Annex III(3) high-risk rules or the Art 5(1)(f) ban?
7. Does SB 243 apply if the AI teacher has a persona or memory?
8. Is Little Duet a CCPA "business" (CAADC scope)? How do Connecticut, Vermont and Nebraska apply given no thresholds?
9. Is a closed family circle a DSA "online platform"? (The Art 19 SME exemption probably applies anyway.)
10. Does EU ePrivacy require consent for first-party aggregated analytics in each target Member State, and what exactly does the UK DUAA exemption cover?
