# Little Duet: curriculum pilot (5 songs), for review

Written by the curriculum-architect agent on 2026-10-16 and checked by `build_lessons.py --check-plan`. Review it before the agent runs the other 95 songs and before any videos are rendered.

**How it works:** each song keeps its tested musical spine (class order, notes, timing). The agent designs the thinking game on top of it: the title, the goal, the skills, the thinking target, what the parent does, what the child does, the sound-to-keys image, the opening lines, up to 2 micro-games, and the after-class line. Parent-facing text names the skill practiced and never promises an outcome; the checker blocks words such as smarter, brain, IQ and neural.

## Twinkle Twinkle Little Star (ages 2-3, 9 classes, 39 min)

_Practices steady beat, high and low, loud and soft, stop and go, and playing together, using a tune your child already knows._

| # | Title | Skill | Thinking target | Parent does | Child does | Image | Games |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Rock the little star | Steady beat | Periodicity (steady pulse) | Hold your child on your lap and rock side to side on every beat. | Sway, clap and pat your knees with the song. | The beat is the star's heartbeat: tap, tap, tap, never in a hurry. | - |
| 2 | Tap the star's heartbeat | Steady beat | One-to-one correspondence | Rest your hand over your child's hand and tap together, then slowly let go. | Tap any key once for each beat. Any key is right. | Each beat is a stepping stone; one tap lands on each stone. | beat: Two taps for twin stars |
| 3 | Bear low, bird high | High and low | Spatial mapping (pitch to place) | Growl and crouch for low sounds; flap your arms and stretch up for high. | Bear sound: tap the left side. Bird sound: tap the right. | Low notes are a heavy bear on the left; high notes are a tiny bird. | listen: Watch the lights walk down |
| 4 | Thunder taps, rain taps | Loud, soft, fast, slow | Opposites (magnitude contrast) | Make big strong arms for loud taps; whisper and tiptoe your fingers for soft. | Loud: big thunder taps. Soft: tiny raindrop taps. | Loud taps are big thunder; soft taps are raindrops on a window. | - |
| 5 | Turtle and bunny | Loud, soft, fast, slow | Invariance under tempo change | Walk your fingers up your child's back, slowly then quickly, matching the music. | Turtle music: slow taps. Bunny music: quick taps. | Slow taps are turtle steps; fast taps are bunny hops across the keys. | beat: Turtle sings the star; beat: Bunny sings the star |
| 6 | Freeze, little star! | Listening memory | Cause and effect (sound, silence) | Freeze with a funny face when the music stops; cheer when it starts again. | Tap while music plays. Freeze like a statue when it stops. | Music on, the star twinkles; music off, the star holds still. | - |
| 7 | Guess the music game | Listening memory | Classification (sorting by feature) | Pause before each picture and ask: bear or bird? Loud or soft? Then cheer. | Guess the game, then tap high, low, loud, soft, slow or fast. | Every sound wears a costume: bear, bird, thunder, raindrop, turtle or bunny. | - |
| 8 | You and me, star duet | Playing together | Interlocking parts (two roles) | Play the tune and watch your child; if they stop, keep playing and smile. | Tap the beat on any key while your grown-up plays the tune. | The tune is the star; your beat is the sky that holds it. | - |
| 9 | Concert for teddy | Song shape | Sequence: beginning, middle, end | Sit teddy as the audience; play the tune, then clap, cheer and bow together. | Tap the beat to the very end, then take a big bow. | The song is a little walk: start at the door, end with a bow. | - |

## Old MacDonald Had a Farm (ages 2-3, 10 classes, 39 min)

_Practices steady beat, high and low, loud and soft, and playing together, with a farm song full of repeats and animal sounds._

| # | Title | Skill | Thinking target | Parent does | Child does | Image | Games |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Wait for E-I-E-I-O | Listening memory | Pattern repetition (returning refrain) | Sway with your child, then lift both arms high on every E-I-E-I-O. | Sway, then wave your arms up for E-I-E-I-O! | E-I-E-I-O walks down the keys like stairs, and it always comes back. | listen: Here comes E-I-E-I-O |
| 2 | Farmer boots on the keys | Steady beat | Periodicity (steady pulse) | Rest your hand on your child's hand and tap together, then let go. | Tap any key like farmer boots: step, step, step. | The beat is the farmer walking: boots go step, step, never in a hurry. | beat: Step, step, step |
| 3 | Bear low, bird high | High and low | Spatial mapping of pitch | Point left and growl for low sounds; point right and chirp for high. | Bear sound? Tap left. Bird sound? Tap right. | Low notes are a heavy bear on the left; high notes, a bird on the right. | high_low: Big bear, down low; high_low: Little bird, up high |
| 4 | Big moo, tiny cheep | Loud, soft, fast, slow | Magnitude contrast (big vs small) | Tap with a strong flat hand for loud, tiny fingertips for soft. Never bang. | Loud like a cow, soft like a sleepy chick. | Loud is a strong cow step on the keys; soft is a chick tiptoeing. | - |
| 5 | Turtle slow, bunny fast | Loud, soft, fast, slow | Rate contrast (slow versus fast) | Tap with your child; if taps get wild, slow down together and breathe. | Slow turtle taps, then quick bunny taps! | Slow taps are turtle steps; fast taps are bunny hops on the keys. | beat: Turtle walks to the farm; beat: Bunny hops to the farm |
| 6 | Freeze like a scarecrow | Listening memory | Inhibitory control (start and stop) | Freeze with your child in a silly scarecrow pose whenever the music stops. | Music on, tap! Music off, freeze! | Music on, hands dance on the keys; music off, hands freeze like scarecrows. | - |
| 7 | Moo moo, tap tap | Counting | One-to-one correspondence (word to tap) | Say the words slowly and tap your child's hand once per word. | Moo moo: two taps, one for each moo. | Each word is one footprint on the keys: moo, moo, two little prints. | listen: Moo moo here, moo moo there |
| 8 | Guess the farm game | Listening memory | Rule switching (sorting by sound) | Before the picture shows, ask: bear or bird? Cheer for every guess. | Listen, guess the game, then play it. | Every sound gives your hands a job: left, right, big, tiny, slow, quick. | - |
| 9 | Farmer and helper | Playing together | Interlocking parts (two roles) | Play the tune; let your child tap the beat and shout E-I-E-I-O. | Tap the beat and shout E-I-E-I-O with your grown-up. | Grown-up hands sing the tune; little hands are farm boots stepping. | - |
| 10 | Farm concert for teddy | Song shape | Sequencing start to end | Seat teddy as the audience, play the song, then bow and cheer together. | Tap the beat to the end, then take a bow! | The last long O is the farm gate closing: hold it, then bow. | - |

## Mary Had a Little Lamb (ages 3-4, 10 classes, 39 min)

_Practices steady beat, finding one special note, echoing short patterns, counting to three and taking turns, with a gentle tune about a lamb._

| # | Title | Skill | Thinking target | Parent does | Child does | Image | Games |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sway with the little lamb | Listening memory | Pattern repetition (A and A) | Sway with your child; when the first tune comes back, gasp and point: again! | Sway with the song and shout "again!" when it repeats. | The lamb follows Mary everywhere, and the tune follows itself back. | - |
| 2 | Little lamb steps | Steady beat | One-to-one correspondence | Rest your hand on your child's hand and tap together, one tap per beat. | Tap any key once for every lamb step. | Each tap is one little hoof on the path: step, step, step. | beat: Slow lamb steps |
| 3 | Find the lamb's note E | Keyboard map | Spatial landmarks | Guide your child's finger over two black keys, then slide right to E. | Touch the two black keys, slide right, and press E gently. | E is the lamb's door, just right of the two black keys. | - |
| 4 | Mama sheep, baby lamb | Keyboard map | Octave equivalence (spatial mapping) | Say "mama" in a deep voice for low E, "baby" in a tiny voice for high E. | Hop between low E and high E, deep voice then tiny voice. | Low E is mama sheep on the left; high E is baby lamb. | high_low: Baby lamb sings up high |
| 5 | Baa-baa echo | Listening memory | Sequence recall (span 2-3) | Play the short call, then fold your hands and wait for your child's answer. | Listen to the lamb's call, then call it back the same. | The lamb calls baa-baa across the field; you call back the same. | - |
| 6 | Down the hill and up | Patterns | Mirror symmetry (contour) | Walk your fingers down your child's arm for E-D-C, back up for C-D-E. | Walk down the hill, then walk back up the same way. | E, D, C: the lamb walks down the hill to the left, then back up. | your_turn: Down the hill: E, D, C; your_turn: Up the hill: C, D, E |
| 7 | Wait for the lamb's E | Playing together | Counting to three | Play your notes; count one, two, three out loud as your child plays E. | Wait for your turn, then play E: one, two, three! | Three Es in a row are three lamb hops: hop, hop, hop. | your_turn: Three hops: one, two, three |
| 8 | Two notes: D and E | Listening memory | Classification into two sets | Point to D or E just before it comes, then let your child press it. | Listen, choose D or E, and press the right one. | D sits between the two black keys; E is next door, on the right. | your_turn: Little lamb: D, D, D |
| 9 | One beat, two players | Playing together | Entrainment (shared pulse) | Nod your head on every beat and keep going, whatever notes happen. | Play your notes and nod along with your grown-up. | You and your grown-up walk the lamb together, side by side. | - |
| 10 | A lamb concert for teddy | Song shape | Form: beginning and ending | Seat teddy as the audience; hold the last C long, then bow together. | Play for teddy, hold the last note, then take a bow. | The last note is low C, home: hold it long like a hug. | - |

## Ode to Joy (ages 4-5, 9 classes, 32 min)

_Practices finding keys by landmarks, mirror patterns, counting beats and the song's shape, while you and your child play Ode to Joy together._

| # | Title | Skill | Thinking target | Parent does | Child does | Image | Games |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Find your five-finger house | Keyboard map | Spatial mapping with a landmark | Point to the two black keys, then help your child rest the thumb on C. | Find the two black keys, then put your thumb on C. | Two black keys are a roof; C is the door just left of it. | your_turn: One finger, one key |
| 2 | Up the stairs, down again | Patterns | Mirror symmetry | Walk two fingers up your child's arm on the way up, then back down. | Fingers three, four, five climb up, then five, four, three come down. | Each key is a stair: step up to G, then step back down. | your_turn: Climb up, climb down |
| 3 | Which line goes home? | Song shape | Same and different (comparison) | After each line, ask: did it land home? Cheer with a hug when it does. | Listen to the ending and pat your lap when it lands on C. | C is the front door: the last note walks right back inside. | listen: Which one goes home? |
| 4 | Little steps, big frog hop | Keyboard map | Interval distance: step versus leap | Show your child the low G outside the house, then help the thumb hop there. | Tiptoe the small steps, then hop your thumb down to low G. | Steps are next-door keys; the frog hop jumps far left, where sounds are low. | listen: Tiny steps, then hop! |
| 5 | Spot the happy tune | Song shape | Pattern repetition (A-B-A form) | Each time 'Happy, happy' starts, hold up one more finger and count aloud. | Play slowly and say the number each time the happy tune returns. | The happy tune is a friend who visits three times; wave hello each time. | - |
| 6 | Walk, walk, run-ning | Counting | Ratio 2:1 (half and double) | Say 'walk' for long notes and 'run-ning' for the two quick ones. | Say walk and run-ning while your fingers play the dance line. | A walking note is one big step; run-ning is two tiny mouse steps. | your_turn: Walk, run-ning, walk, walk |
| 7 | Four beats, slow and even | Steady beat | Grouping by four (meter) | Tap your child's shoulder on each beat and count 1, 2, 3, 4 softly. | Play slowly and evenly while you count to four together. | Each bar is a little train with four cars: one, two, three, four. | beat: Tap and count to four |
| 8 | Keep up with the band | Playing together | Entrainment (matching a tempo) | Sing the words along and nod on each beat; if a note slips, keep going. | Play with the music and keep going, like a train on its track. | The music is a moving train; hop on and ride, even past bumps. | - |
| 9 | Concert for teddy | Loud, soft, fast, slow | Ordinal sequence (start, middle, end) | Sit teddy as the audience; announce the song, then clap and bow together at the end. | Bow, play the whole song for teddy, then bow again. | Play it like a happy parade: proud start, bouncy middle, home at the end. | - |

## Fur Elise (ages 5-6, 10 classes, 37 min)

_Practices keyboard landmarks, see-saw patterns, counting in sixes, octaves and two-hand teamwork, using a famous tune about a little bird._

| # | Title | Skill | Thinking target | Parent does | Child does | Image | Games |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Find the bird's perch | Keyboard map | Landmark-based spatial anchoring | Point to the two black keys together, then help your child slide the thumb just right of them. | Find the white key right after two black keys. That's E! | Two black keys are a little roof; E sits on the doorstep just to the right. | demo: One finger, one key |
| 2 | Three hops and a long sit | Counting | Ratio 3:1 (short to long) | Say hop, hop, hop, sit with the notes, and tap your child's knee once on each hop. | Hop up three keys, then sit on the long one. | The bird hops up the branch, one, two, three, then sits still and sings. | listen: Count the hops up the branch |
| 3 | Two endings, one song | Song shape | Repetition with variation (A, A') | Wave when the bird tune comes back. Sing the next word if your child gets stuck. | Wave hello when the bird tune comes back again. | The first ending flies up like a question; the second flies home to A. | listen: Up like a question, down home |
| 4 | The see-saw notes | Finger control | Alternating pattern (A-B-A-B-A) | Rock gently side to side with your child: lean right on E, left on D sharp. | See-saw between the white E and the black key. Count the E's! | Pinky on white E, ring finger on the black key: up, down, up, down. | your_turn: See-saw slowly: E, black, E |
| 5 | Low A, the bird's tree | Keyboard map | Octave translation (same note, lower) | Play the tune while your child plays low A. Count the bars out loud: one, two, three. | Play low A with your left pinky at the start of each bar. | High A is the bird; low A is the tree trunk, same name, deeper sound. | listen: Same name, lower house |
| 6 | Count to six, land on one | Steady beat | Grouping in sixes (periodicity) | Count ONE-two-three-four-five-six with each bar, loud on ONE. Keep going if notes slip. | Play low A on every big ONE. Keep going, no stopping! | Six little bird hops, then one deep thump from the tree. Again! | - |
| 7 | Tree and bird together | Finger control | Ratio 1:6 (two streams) | Rest a hand on your child's left shoulder and say tree on each low A. Go very slowly. | Left hand holds low A while right hand hops the tune. | Left hand is the tree, still and low; right hand is the bird hopping. | - |
| 8 | Slow bird, quick bird | Loud, soft, fast, slow | Tempo scaling (same pattern, faster) | Flap your arms slowly for slow music, quicker for quick music. Go back to slow if needed. | Play the bird song slow, then a little quicker. | A sleepy bird and a busy bird sing the very same song. | listen: The sleepy bird sings; listen: Now the busy bird sings |
| 9 | Fly with the music | Playing together | Synchrony with a moving pulse | Sit beside your child and nod on every big ONE. Cheer for keeping going, not for notes. | Fly along with the music. If a note slips, keep flying! | The music is the wind; the bird keeps flapping and rides along. | - |
| 10 | The bird's concert | Song shape | Sequencing and closure (ending home) | Seat teddy as the audience. Clap, cheer and bow together at the end. | Play the bird song for teddy, end softly, then bow. | The bird flies away softly and lands home on A. The end! | your_turn: Fly away softly, land on A |

## Points for you to decide (raised by the agent)

1. **Old MacDonald, class 3:** the video captions say "Bear!" and "Bird!" because the spine is shared, so the agent kept bear and bird instead of farm animals. v2 could let the agent re-theme the spine's captions per song (cow and chick), at the cost of a larger check.
2. **Old MacDonald, class 7:** counting is the main skill at ages 2-3, kept within the limit of counting to 2 ("two moos, two taps"). The other option is Patterns.
3. **Ode to Joy, class 4:** the leap down to G3 leaves the five-finger position. A teacher should confirm it, or the parent can play that note.
4. **Für Elise, classes 5-7:** the spine's left hand is a single A3 throughout. That is easy, but E3 fits the harmony better in one phrase. This is a musical change for the spine, not for the agent.
5. **Twinkle, class 9:** the main skill is labelled "Song shape" to widen the skill map. "Playing together" may be the more honest label at ages 2-3.
